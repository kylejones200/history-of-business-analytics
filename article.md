### History of Business Analytics
#### Analytics is one of those business terms that seems like the ultimate miracle cure for managers who want to improve business performance. But like most technologies, the promise cannot be fulfilled unless managers first understand how and when it should be used.


<figcaption>Photo by Museums Victoria on Unsplash</figcaption>


Data analysis software programs have been for more than 40 years.
Comshare promoted decision support systems in the 1970s whose purpose
was to take data and provide end users with the ability to manipulate
and summarize data from multiple business dimensions. These systems
include predictive capabilities based on statistical analysis, as well
as software 'agents' that search the data and show exceptional trends
for the attention of users, without having to dig into the data.

However, three factors are emerging within analytics that make modern
analytics essential for making good decisions.

Data availability: Bernard Marr, author and Forbes columnist, refers to
the "complete datafication of our world," which he believes will provide
"insights on everything." At no other time in history have companies had
so much data collected or available at their fingertips. Whether it is
sales information, market predictions or comments received on social
media, they all offer raw material on which decisions can be based.

> "Analytics is something that any manager, leader or indeed anyone
> should know. It is difficult to think of any aspect of life that is
> not affected by analytics." --- Bernard Marr

Technology capabilities: Analytical technologies have advanced
considerably in recent years. Advanced capabilities were normally
restricted to analyzing the numbers that were presented by the system as
a block of data. Today's systems include text analysis, predictive
capabilities that can show correlations between different data sets to
produce more accurate forecasts, and real-time processing so that
decisions can be automated and executed in an instant. Support for 'Big
Data' also means that the amount of data is not too much and it can
cover a wide variety of formats.

Accessibility: Analytical capabilities are now built into all classes of
systems, from Accounting to end-user spreadsheets, typically supplied as
low-cost or free 'extra'. You no longer need to buy specialized systems
to take advantage of the latest analytics news, while 'ease of use' has
eradicated the need for users to become experts in statistics or
scientific data in order to take advantage of them.

Since the 2013 edition, Microsoft Excel has a columnar database engine
(PowerPivot) that can handle millions of rows or data in memory, which
means that all Excel users have more power to analyze data from the that
was possible in the past.

However, these three factors alone do not translate into business
benefits directly. Analytics is just a tool and like any other tool, it
can be both valuable and harmful. Valuable in that when applied
correctly it can help organizations discover things about the way they
operate that can be used to reduce costs or increase sales. Harmful in
that, if used wrongly, it may be a waste of management time, but worst,
it can lead organizations to make the wrong decisions.

Hans Peter Luhn, German computer scientist from IBM, coined the term
"Business Intelligence" in 1958 in an article titled "A Business
Intelligence System" in which he described a system that stores
information in textual documents.

> Business Intelligence is "the ability to apprehend the
> interrelationships of the facts presented in such a way that they
> guide the action towards a desired objective." --- Hans Peter Luhn

A couple of decades later, Richard Kimball and Bill Inmon revolutionized
the architecture of databases for analytics and we largely still use
their design today.

As early as 2000, another type of more modern, proactive, fast and
efficient business analytics emerged, which needs Business Intelligence
as the basis for its statistical calculations and inferences: Business
Analytics.

Regarding Big Data (management and storage of large amounts of data), it
is concerned that, without being analytical (based on the purity of the
term), and together with the cheaper hardware, they have made it
possible to increase data samples, Analytics Cognitive (based on Machine
Learning) and the evolution of platforms and paradigms such as IoT that,
today, help our businesses in all possible sectors to our
decision-making processes. What does this technology do if it doesn't
reduce the amount of uncertainties in order to make better decisions?

> "The intelligence of the individual is measured by the amount of
> uncertainty that is capable of bearing." --- Inmanuel Kant, the
> Prussian philosopher

A central pillar of a digital company is data driven decision making.
Meaning leaders use data to improve business outcomes. This trend is
part of the natural path of digital transformation, leading
organizations towards a new digital culture.

This situation is giving rise to a number of trends in the field of
analytics and business intelligence that leaders should consider when
developing their digital investment strategies. Gartner, a consultancy,
reports that the five most important factors for a data driven company
are automating data analytics workloads, developing a new digital
culture, relationship analytics, decision intelligence, and scaling up
analytics operations and use of analytical data.

#### Machine learning to speed up analytics
Automation is being applied to an increasing number of data analysis
operations , in order to obtain results faster and without the need for
human intervention. This, known as augmented analytics, involves
developing machine learning models that are capable of analyzing and
understanding the data generated in the business to extract conclusions
with more agility.

All this results in greater investment by companies in IT
infrastructures and services that allow the application of machine
learning techniques and models in various fields of analytics. For
example, in the preparation of data to later extract valuable knowledge,
such as behavior patterns in customers and consumers, which allow to
refine strategies to improve the customer experience. This technology
also serves to automate a growing volume of data science techniques and
the development of increasingly complex machine learning models, which
also allows the exchange of knowledge between different profiles of
business users, employees linked to the analytics and data scientists of
the companies. According to Gartner researchers,

This evolutionary leap will begin to have more visibility this year, and
will provide valuable information to more members of the business
network, from managers to workers and partners. A path that will lead
organizations to develop analytical models and approaches that further
avoid bias in the interpretation of the data collected by the
organization. According to experts, the advancement of augmented
analytics will give impetus to the profession of "data scientist".

#### A new digital culture
Successful completion of digital transformation requires organizations
to take an important step in terms of their traditional corporate
culture , which will be displaced by a digital one. This forces
organizations to ensure they have the digital skills necessary to work
with data and business intelligence at all levels of the business. But
it is also necessary to understand how new digital technologies behave
in relation to increasingly sensitive aspects, such as ethics and
privacy.

Experts are already talking about digital literacy, one of the great
pending issues in companies and in society in general. Because, to adopt
technologies such as advanced analytics or business intelligence as they
are understood now, it is necessary that the workers and managers of the
organization have a deeper understanding of the new technologies and
their implications.

As a result of this change, those who manage to become digitally
literate will have a tool that will not only serve them for their work,
but for life in modern society. Because currently the vast majority of
the population, including digital natives, constantly suffer from the
inability to identify problems and threats of the digital age, such as
false information circulating through Fake News, fake videos and all
kinds of fraudulent information, that many take for true.

According to Gartner, by 2023 60% of organizations with more than 20
data scientists will need to implement a clear code of conduct that
avoids problems such as bias or the use of potentially false
information. And this affects all the data that reaches the company and
is analyzed to obtain business intelligence since, if the information is
not properly screened, the conclusions obtained from it will be false.
Therefore, it is recommended to promote internal discourse on digital
ethics and good practices in obtaining and processing information.

#### Relationship analytics to understand the digital ecosystem
Data needs context to be valuable. This has made the analytical
techniques and intelligence applied to this field evolve a lot, until a
new paradigm called relationship analytics has been reached. It is a way
of interrelating the data based on all the links that unite them, taking
into account aspects such as location, graphic similarities or the
relationships that can be established in social matters.

All of this allows for a better understanding of how people, places and
things are connected, in an era in which most of the valuable
information is contained in unstructured data. Due to this, experts
affirm that from this year there will be important advances in
relationship analytics, in the search for new and better ways to
associate data of interest to business. This will improve the accuracy
of predictions used in business intelligence and operational and
commercial decision making.

Gartner highlights that each type of relational analytics provides
advantages in certain fields. For example, procedures based on graph
analysis can allow real-time identification of illegal behavior and
criminal activity. The analysis of formal and informal networks of
people would allow agencies to identify certain crimes such as money
laundering and other criminal activities that are less obvious or that
do not leave a mark on images.

#### Decision intelligence
When trying to apply the results of analytics to business intelligence,
organizations run into problems, since the origin of the data is
constantly changing, which requires advanced techniques to manage,
analyze and understand the information adequately. Hence the problem
that decision models based on analytics data can become unpredictable,
making it difficult for decision makers to rely on analytical data.

Given this, decision intelligence is presented, which provides a common
pool for traditional and more advanced analytical techniques, in which
it is possible to design, model, monitor, modify, apply and review at
all times the models in which and builds analytics and subsequent
data-driven decision making. This year will see a significant advance in
this new trend, driven by the need to deal with the constant
fluctuations of business digital ecosystems and their changing data
sources.
